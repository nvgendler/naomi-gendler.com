Naomi Gendler

Member, School of Natural Sciences
Institute for Advanced Study, Princeton

I am a theoretical physicist working on string theory and what it predicts for the physics we can observe.

My research asks how the geometry of the extra dimensions shapes low-energy physics, and how experiments can test it. I compute the masses, couplings and cosmological consequences of axions across large ensembles of explicit Calabi–Yau compactifications; I test proposed quantum-gravity constraints on effective field theories; and I develop new mathematical and computational methods for studying Calabi–Yau geometry.

Before joining the IAS, I was a postdoctoral fellow at Harvard University. I received my Ph.D. from Cornell University in 2023, advised by Liam McAllister, after an M.Sc. in applied mathematics at the University of Auckland and a B.A. in physics at Reed College.
